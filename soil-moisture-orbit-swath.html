How the satellite actually covers the Earth

A soil-moisture radiometer sees only a 1000-km stripe directly beneath it. It builds a global map by circling the planet ~14.6 times a day while the Earth turns underneath — so any one place is only visited every 2–3 days. Play it forward and watch the gaps. Everything between passes is interpolated.

Why this matters: the “daily global soil-moisture map” isn’t observed daily everywhere. Between overpasses each pixel is filled by interpolation or model assimilation — a temporal inference sitting on top of every spatial one.
