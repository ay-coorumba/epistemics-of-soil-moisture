The same glow, heard at the wrong frequency — and read behind glass.

China and Russia listen to the same thermal glow SMAP does — but at X-band, where the soil speaks from only its top centimetre, the canopy is a wall, and no law protects the silence. Then the two journeys fork institutionally, in opposite directions: 风云三号 FengYun-3 compensates with constellation scale and machine-learned retrievals; Метеор-М Meteor-M treats soil moisture as a by-product — from the nation that invented spaceborne microwave radiometry, and whose dielectric physics quietly sits inside everyone else’s pipeline.

The pedigree includes the auditor

The physics on this page is the same physics as the rest of the collection — Fresnel emissivity, τ–ω, dielectric mixing. What differs is everything wrapped around it: the frequency a nation could afford to fly, the retrieval it chose to trust, and — decisively — who is allowed to check. A number nobody outside the building can audit is not a worse number; it is a different kind of number, and the ~90%-Western global record is the sum of exactly such differences.

And one quiet inversion to hold onto: while Russian satellite data rarely leaves home, Russian science is everywhere — the Mironov dielectric model inside SMAP’s own retrieval chain is the direct descendant of the programme that put the first microwave radiometer in orbit in 1968. Data has borders. Physics doesn’t.

Worked for FengYun-3 MWRI (X-band, 10.65 GHz, CMA/CNSA/CAS) and Meteor-M MTVZA-GYa (Roscosmos/Roshydromet). Physics is standard (skin depth, vegetation opacity scaling, multi-frequency indices); FY-3 retrieval and recalibration details follow the published literature (random-forest retrievals, FY-3B/C/D record harmonisation), and MTVZA soil-moisture use follows the published Arctic experiments. Institutional characterisations are the collection’s own editorial reading — see Four Flags, Four Pipelines for the sourced comparison. Worked numbers and per-step σ values are illustrative magnitudes.
