What “validated” really means

A satellite pixel is “validated” by comparing it to probes in the ground. But a probe samples a few cm³ at one spot, while the pixel averages ~81 km². They don’t measure the same thing — so much of the “error” in a validation isn’t the satellite being wrong, it’s the probe failing to represent the pixel. Add probes and watch the real satellite error emerge from underneath the mismatch.

A statistical teaching model: the network estimate of the pixel mean has standard error σ_hetero/√n, so apparent validation RMSE ≈ √(σ_sat² + σ_hetero²/n). Values are illustrative. In reality the true pixel mean is never known, which is why methods like triple collocation estimate satellite error without treating any reference as truth.
